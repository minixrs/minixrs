## On the two named-but-unproven orderings

Both are acceptable to ship. `do_trunc`'s free-after-write-back and `create`'s dirent-after-`write_inode` each need a failure *strictly between* two steps, and the only failure available there is `EIO` from `Blocks::read`/`write` — whose sole source is the `memory` ramdisk, a fixed RAM mapping whose block numbers are bounds-checked by `write_zone_ok`/`zone_ok`/`inode_location` before they leave MFS. No probe can induce one, and nothing short of an injectable fault device would change that. Both also fail in the recoverable direction, so the cost of being wrong is a leak. Naming them in `CLAUDE.md` and at the call site is the right disposition — which is exactly why I2(a) matters: one of the two is named everywhere except the function it governs.
